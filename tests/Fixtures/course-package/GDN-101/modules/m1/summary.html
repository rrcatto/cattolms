• Roots need water, air and food from the soil, and most garden failures start there.
  • A good loam is about 45% mineral particles, 25% water, 25% air and 5% organic matter.
  • Sand drains fast and holds little, clay holds water but drains slowly, and loam is a balance of sand, silt and clay.
  • A jar test shows your soil's texture: sand settles first, then silt, then clay.
  • Most vegetables grow best between pH 6.0 and 7.0. Test before adding lime or sulphur.
  • Compost needs roughly two to three parts browns to one part greens, kept as damp as a wrung-out sponge and turned every week or two.
  • A container must be big enough for its crop, drain freely, be safe for food, and be filled with potting soil, never garden soil.
  • Mulch and deep, early-morning watering keep a small garden alive through dry spells.

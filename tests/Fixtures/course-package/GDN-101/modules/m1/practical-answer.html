Task 1: Do a jar test on your soil

A good answer looks like this: "Settled soil 9 cm deep. Sand 5 cm (56%), silt 2.5 cm (28%), clay 1.5 cm (17%). This is a sandy loam: it will drain well but dry out quickly, so I will dig in compost each season and mulch."

Check your work:

  • Your shares add up to about 100%. If they add up to much less, you measured before the clay had settled: leave the jar another day.
  • You named your soil from the shares: mostly sand is a sandy soil, mostly clay a clay soil, and no one part dominating is a loam.
  • Your action fits your soil. Any soil benefits from compost. A sandy soil also needs mulch and more frequent watering. A clay soil needs compost worked in and should never be dug while wet.

Task 2: Plan a compost bin for your household

A model plan: "Our kitchen makes about two buckets of peels and coffee grounds a week. The bin, a 200-litre plastic compost bin with a lid, goes in the shady corner by the back wall, on bare soil. Browns: dry leaves from the street trees, collected in autumn and kept in bags, and shredded cardboard boxes. We will add five buckets of browns a week, mixed in as we add greens. We water it when it feels dry and turn it every Saturday."

Check your work:

  • Your browns are two to three times your greens by volume. Five buckets of browns to two of greens is two and a half to one.
  • You have a steady source of browns for the whole year, not only in autumn. Storing leaves in bags is the usual answer.
  • Your bin is out of full sun and wind, so it does not dry out, and on soil or a tray so it can drain.
  • Nothing from the "keep out" column is in your plan.

Task 3: Choose and fill one container

A model note: "Container: food-grade plastic tub, 40 cm across and 35 cm deep, with six drainage holes drilled in the base. Filled to 32 cm, its volume is about 3.14 × 20 × 20 × 32 = 40,200 cm³, or 40 litres. Crop: one tomato plant, which needs at least 20 litres and 30 cm depth. Bought one 30 dm³ bag of potting soil and mixed in a 10-litre bucket of compost, 40 litres in all. Watered until it drained, then mulched with 3 cm of straw kept away from the centre."

Check your work:

  • Your container meets the depth and volume in the lesson's table for your crop. For a round container, the volume is 3.14 × radius × radius × the depth of the soil.
  • It has drainage holes, and it stands on feet or blocks so they are not sealed against the floor.
  • You used potting soil, not garden soil, and it is made of a food-safe material.
  • The mulch is 2 to 3 cm deep and does not touch the plant's stem.
